What is house edge?

The house edge is the casino's built-in advantage on every game. Understanding it helps you judge bonuses, wagering requirements and rakeback offers properly.

By the BonusVault editorial teamUpdated November 2026

House edge in one sentence

The house edge is the percentage of each bet the casino expects to keep on average over the long run.

Example

A game with a 2% house edge returns on average $98 for every $100 wagered. Over a single session anything can happen, but over thousands of bets results move towards that average. The same idea is often shown as RTP (return to player): a 2% house edge equals 98% RTP.

Typical ranges

Game typeTypical house edge
Blackjack (good strategy)Around 0.5–1%
Baccarat (banker bet)Around 1%
European roulette2.7%
American roulette5.26%
SlotsOften 2–6% (RTP 94–98%)
Crypto 'originals' (dice, crash)Often around 1%

Exact numbers vary by game and rules – many games show their RTP in the game info.

Why house edge matters for bonuses

When you clear a wagering requirement, your expected cost is roughly the total wagered times the house edge. Our wagering calculator shows this. Rakeback works the other way: it gives you back part of the house edge – try the rakeback calculator.

FAQ

What is a good house edge?

Lower is better for players. Games around 1% or less, like blackjack with good strategy or many crypto originals, are among the lowest.

Is house edge the same as RTP?

They're two sides of the same number. RTP is what's returned to players on average; house edge is what the casino keeps. 97% RTP = 3% house edge.

Can you beat the house edge?

Not over the long run in standard casino games. Rakeback and cashback can reduce your cost, but don't remove the edge.
